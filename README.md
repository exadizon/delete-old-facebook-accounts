# How to Delete Old Facebook Accounts

A step-by-step guide to removing a Facebook account you no longer have access to using Facebook's identity verification process.

---

## Overview

If you have an old Facebook account that you can't access — or you want to remove an account that was created using your identity — you can report it to Facebook as an impersonation and request its deletion by submitting a government-issued ID. Facebook will then review and delete the account.

---

## Steps

### 1. Go to the Facebook Help Form

Open the following link in your browser:

**[https://m.facebook.com/help/contact/295309487309948](https://m.facebook.com/help/contact/295309487309948)**

> This is Facebook's official form for reporting accounts that are impersonating you.

---

### 2. Answer "No" When Asked If You Have a Facebook Account

When the form asks **"Do you have a Facebook account?"**, select **No** — even if you currently have one.

> This is the key step. By saying "No," you are telling Facebook that someone else created an account using your identity without your knowledge or permission. This triggers their impersonation removal process.

---

### 3. Upload a Government-Issued ID

You will be asked to upload a photo ID to verify your identity. Accepted forms of ID typically include:

- Driver's licence
- Passport
- National identity card
- Birth certificate (in some regions)

> **Tip:** If the upload is not accepted on the first try, keep attempting — the form can be finicky. It will work eventually.

---

### 4. Fill In the Additional Info Field

When the form includes a free-text field for additional information, use it to clearly describe why the account is harmful or violates Facebook's Community Standards. Be specific and factual. Examples of things to mention:

- **Content involving minors** – e.g. photos of children posted without consent, or grooming behaviour
- **Nudity or sexual content** – e.g. explicit images shared without permission
- **Self-harm or suicide content** – e.g. posts that encourage or glorify self-harm
- **Harassment or bullying** – e.g. targeted threats, hate speech, or repeated hostile messages directed at a person
- **Impersonation** – e.g. using your name, photos, or personal details to deceive others
- **Spam or fake identity** – e.g. a fabricated account using your likeness

> **Tip:** The more clearly you connect the account's content to a specific Community Standards violation, the stronger your report will be. Facebook reviewers are more likely to act on reports that explain the harm in concrete terms.

---

### 5. Submit the Form

Once your ID is uploaded and the additional info is filled in, submit the form. Facebook will review your report and delete the account associated with your identity.

---

## Why This Works

By going through this flow, you are effectively telling Facebook:

> *"I do not have a Facebook account, but someone is using my identity on the platform."*

Facebook then treats the account as an impersonation of a real person (you), verifies your identity via the uploaded ID, and removes the fraudulent account.

---

## Notes

- This process works for accounts you created years ago and no longer have login access to.
- It also works if someone else created an account using your name/photos.
- This process typically takes ~30 minutes for the email to be received, or in some occasions longer.
- You will not need to log in to any Facebook account to complete this process.

---

## If Your Report Is Rejected

You may receive an email from Facebook that looks like this:

> Hi,
>
> Thanks for letting us know about something you think may go against our Community Standards. Reports like yours are an important part of making Facebook a safe and welcoming environment for everyone. In this case, we reviewed the profile you reported and found that it doesn't go against our Community Standards.
>
> Note: If you see something on someone's profile that shouldn't be on Facebook, be sure to report the specific content (ex: a photo or video), instead of the entire profile. This will help us review your report more accurately.
>
> For more information about our policies, please read the Facebook Community Standards:
> https://www.facebook.com/communitystandards?ref=cr
>
> Thanks,
> The Facebook Team

### What to Do Next

This rejection does **not** mean the process has failed. Here is what to try:

1. **Report specific content instead of the whole profile.** As Facebook's email suggests, find individual posts, photos, or videos on the account and report each one separately. Reports on specific pieces of content are reviewed more carefully than profile-level reports.

2. **Re-submit the impersonation form with a stronger description.** Go back to the form at [https://m.facebook.com/help/contact/295309487309948](https://m.facebook.com/help/contact/295309487309948) and this time include a detailed description in the additional info field. Clearly state *which* Community Standard is being violated (e.g. impersonation, content involving minors, nudity, harassment) and why. See the [Fill In the Additional Info Field](#4-fill-in-the-additional-info-field) section above for guidance.

3. **Try again.** Facebook's review teams process a high volume of reports. A second or third submission — especially one with more detail — can result in a different outcome.

4. **Contact Facebook support directly** at [https://www.facebook.com/help/](https://www.facebook.com/help/) and explain that you are the real person behind the identity being used on the reported account.

> **Remember:** Persistence matters. Many people succeed on a second or third attempt after refining their report description.
